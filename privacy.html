Privacy Policy

Effective 10 October 2026

Trend Shorts Bot ("the app") is a personal automation tool run by its owner (GitHub user BaileyGS7) for the owner's own TikTok account and the owner's own YouTube channel. It is a personal tool, not a service offered to the public, and only the owner can connect an account to it. This policy explains what data the app accesses, why, where it is kept, for how long, and how to remove it.

Data the app accesses from TikTok

Only from the owner's own TikTok account, and only after the owner authorises it on TikTok's login screen:

  • Basic profile information (user.info.basic): open ID, display name and avatar. Used only to confirm which account is connected.
  • Permission to upload and publish videos (video.upload, video.publish): used to send the owner's own finished videos to the account, as drafts for the owner to finish in the TikTok app or as posts. TikTok returns an ID for each upload, which the app keeps in its run history.

The app does not read the owner's TikTok video list or any TikTok view, like, comment or share counts: TikTok's video.list permission is not enabled for the app. If it is ever enabled, this policy will be updated first. The app does not access other users' accounts, messages or followers' data.

Data the app accesses from YouTube (Google)

Only for the owner's own YouTube channel, and only after the owner authorises it on Google's sign-in screen. The app asks for two permissions:

  • Upload videos (https://www.googleapis.com/auth/youtube.upload). The app uses it only to upload the owner's own finished videos as YouTube Shorts (YouTube Data API, videos.insert). It sends the video file with its title, description, tags, category, language, privacy status, a "not made for kids" setting and the "altered or synthetic content" label. Of what Google returns, the app uses and keeps only the new video's ID (and its link) and the privacy and upload status YouTube reports.
  • View YouTube Analytics reports (https://www.googleapis.com/auth/yt-analytics.readonly). The app reads per-video view counts and watch-time statistics of the owner's own videos that it uploaded in the last 22 days (YouTube Analytics API, reports.query): views, engaged views, average view duration, average percentage viewed, and the numbers of likes, shares, comments and subscribers gained. It reads them before making each new video and works out summary figures per video style of its own (format, ending and length). Only the format figures change anything: the AI script writer is told to prefer, among the formats it is offered, those that viewers watch for longest. Endings and lengths keep rotating whatever the figures say; their figures are only shown to the owner.

The app does not read anything else from the owner's YouTube channel or Google account: no other channels, no comment text or commenters, no subscriber details and no Google account profile. It does not change or delete anything on YouTube after uploading.

The app's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. The app uses YouTube API Services: see the YouTube Terms of Service, and the Google Privacy Policy for how Google handles data.

What is stored, and where

  • Logins. The TikTok and Google refresh tokens are stored as encrypted GitHub Actions secrets in the owner's private GitHub repository. They are never written to logs or files. Short-lived access tokens are only used while a run is going.
  • YouTube data: only in a temporary store, not in the code repository. For each video the app uploaded, it keeps the YouTube video ID, the app's own notes on how the video was made (its title, format, ending and length) and the statistics listed above. This store is kept in GitHub Actions' cache, which passes it from one run to the next, outside the repository's files and history. Every run deletes the records of videos uploaded more than 22 days earlier, re-reads the statistics of the others, saves a new copy and deletes the older copies. GitHub automatically deletes a copy that has not been used for 7 days, so no YouTube data is kept for more than 30 days, also while the app is paused.
  • Upload links in run logs. The link to each new YouTube upload is also shown in that run's private log and summary on GitHub, and in the run's downloadable summary file, so the owner can find the video. GitHub deletes run logs after 30 days and the downloadable files after 14 days.
  • Summary of what works. From the statistics, the app works out summary figures per category of its own (format, ending and length), only for categories with at least 5 measured videos: how many videos were measured, the median percentage watched, the median time watched and the median number of views. This summary contains no video IDs, links or titles. Every run works it out again; it is kept only next to the statistics in the temporary store and deleted with them, never in the repository. The owner also sees it in the app's private run summaries on GitHub, which GitHub deletes after 30 days.
  • Run history. The app's own record of each run (the trend, the title and caption it wrote, how the video was made, the TikTok upload IDs, whether a YouTube upload worked with the privacy status YouTube reported and, for a failed upload, its error message, and the estimated AI cost) is kept in the private repository (the newest 500 runs). It does not contain YouTube video IDs, links or statistics.
  • Videos. Finished videos are kept as private workflow files and are deleted automatically after 14 days.

Services used to make videos

To create videos, the app uses Anthropic's Claude (web research and script writing), Google Gemini (backup script writing, used only when Claude can't be), Google Trends and Reddit (public lists of what's trending), Pixabay (stock footage; also Pexels if a Pexels key is added), Microsoft text-to-speech (the voiceover; ElevenLabs instead if the owner sets it up) and GitHub (the private repository, its secrets, automation and run logs, and this website).

The AI script writer (Claude) receives public trend information, the titles and hooks of the app's own recent videos, and the summary figures per format described above, for formats with at least 5 measured videos (for example "explainer videos: 70% watched on average, 6 videos"). The backup writer (Gemini, used only when Claude can't be) receives the same request without those figures: nothing received from YouTube's APIs is ever sent to Gemini. No TikTok or YouTube video IDs, links, per-video statistics, tokens or account details are sent to the AI writers or to the services that supply trends, footage and voices.

Sharing and selling

The app does not sell, rent or share TikTok or Google data with anyone beyond what is described above, does not use it for advertising, and does not allow humans to read it except the owner managing their own account.

Cookies and third parties

This website and the app use no cookies, no analytics or tracking, no advertising and no third-party content. The website's pages load only their own files.

Retention and deletion

  • TikTok access can be revoked at any time in the TikTok app under Settings and privacy → Security & permissions → Apps and services permissions.
  • In addition to the app's normal procedure for deleting stored data, you can revoke the app's access to your Google account at any time via the Google security settings page at https://security.google.com/settings/security/permissions.
  • The app's Disconnect YouTube step revokes the app's access with Google immediately, deletes the saved login and deletes the stored YouTube data, including the summary of what works. If access is revoked on Google's page instead, the app's next run finds that its login no longer works and deletes the stored YouTube data then; if the app is not running, GitHub deletes it within 7 days.
  • The app deletes each video's YouTube data from its store once it is 22 days old, and no copy of it is kept for more than 30 days, as described above.
  • Requests to delete data are completed within 7 days. This includes the run history, which is otherwise kept while the app is in use and deleted when the owner deletes the repository.

Children

The app is not directed at children and does not knowingly process children's data. Every YouTube upload is marked as not made for kids.
